# KLUNK: IP review of the retention names and the LG3 renames, 2026-09-26

Reviewer: legal-reviewer (KLUNK studio). This review follows on from [`ip-review-2026-09-26.md`](ip-review-2026-09-26.md). It covers every name in `docs/RETENTION.md` marked **[LP] / pending legal** (lane names, trophies, mission texts, Journey rewards, set-mastery items, Aquarium items, stop-moment copy; EN and SV) and the renames queued in `docs/BACKLOG.md` **LG3**.

> **This is a risk review, not legal advice.** It flags collisions and close calls and proposes low-cost fixes. Section 7 lists what a lawyer should confirm.

Method: web search for each name that could plausibly collide (game titles, other games' feature names, characters, brands and celebrities), plus general knowledge for the rest. Risk means how likely the name is to draw a complaint, a store-review problem or a reputational "copycat" reading. Legal risk of infringement is lower than that for almost every item here, because nearly all the names are ordinary descriptive words used inside the app.

### 2.4 Journey: assessment

- **Journey (2012)**, developed by thatgamecompany and published by Sony, is a famous game title. Its store and credits pages state that "Journey" is a trademark of Sony Interactive Entertainment. It is also on iOS and Steam.
- **Angry Birds Journey** (Rovio) is a current mobile game title, and its **paid** season track is called the **"Journey Pass"**. Many 7–10-year-olds will know it.
- Our use is different in kind: the ordinary English word used **inside the app** as the label of a free progression track, never as our title. That is descriptive use, and a genre, look and store title with no overlap with either game. Legal risk is low.
- The real risk is association: "Journey" plus a reward track is exactly Rovio's naming for a premium pass, which clashes with our "no premium lane" promise.

**Risk: LOW** if these guards hold:
1. Never "Journey Pass", "Season Journey" or any premium or seasonal framing.
2. Never use "Journey" in the store title, subtitle, keywords, icon or screenshot captions as a feature brand. In store text, describe it in lowercase ("a long reward path that never resets").
3. No visual nods to Journey (2012): no robed figure with a flowing scarf, no desert dunes or glowing glyphs on the track.
4. Trophies "Journey 10 / Resan 10" and "Journey 50 / Resan 50" follow whatever the lane is called.

If the lawyer or producer prefers zero overlap, rename to **Pearl Path / Pärlstigen**. It is cheap now and costly after launch.

## 7. What a lawyer should confirm before the Batch B store release

1. **Pirate buddy:** that "Captain Anchor / Kapten Ankare" clears Playmobil (Captain Kip) and Kapten Sabeltand (Pelle), and whether Kapten Sabeltand's owner holds Nordic marks on character names.
2. **Ice set:** that "The Snowglows / Isbitarna" is clear, and that dropping "Icelings" is enough (Disney Junior).
3. **Pearl Pool:** a registry check for PEARL POOL and TIDEPOOL in classes 9/41 (EU, SE, US), and whether the indie *Tidepool* daily game has filed a mark.
4. **Journey:** confirm that an in-app feature label "Journey" is non-infringing descriptive use against Sony's JOURNEY and Rovio's ANGRY BIRDS JOURNEY / "Journey Pass", given the guards in §2.4. Or decide to switch to Pearl Path.
5. **Store text (CS2):** that the lane names used in the listing (Daily Jar, Pearl Pool, Daily Present) raise no issue, and that the "no streaks, no timers, nothing expires" promises stay accurate.
6. The earlier review's §9 items (KLUNK clearance, Suika, open-source notices, privacy, loot-box and PEGI questions, AI-assisted authorship) still apply.
